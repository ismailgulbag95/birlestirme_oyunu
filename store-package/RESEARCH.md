# Store research and product positioning

Research snapshot: 30 September 2026. Store ranks, ratings, and download estimates change over time; the linked live listings are the source of truth.

## What the current game actually does

- Starts with Fire, Water, Earth, and Air.
- The player chooses two discovered items and places them at a 3D alchemist's workbench to try a combination.
- A successful recipe reveals a new item; the discovery count and collection grow.
- A hint system and a collection/codex interface are present. The workshop contains a seated character, shelves, a wooden table, glowing pads, and 3D item tokens.
- The Classic data set defines 92 items and 88 two-item recipes. The source has no three-item recipes, even though some UI copy suggests otherwise.
- Room presentation milestones exist at 10 and 40 discoveries. Store art uses the 40-discovery collection state without claiming a new room scene that the capture does not visibly prove.
- The Grandmaster set is gated in the UI, but live subscription and rewarded-ad providers are not configured in this checkout. Listing copy therefore focuses on Classic mode and makes no monetization promise.

### Core loop and strongest moment

1. Pick up to two already discovered items from the inventory.
2. Place them on the two glowing workbench pads.
3. Press the combine control; a valid recipe produces a new item, while an unknown pair gets a fail animation.
4. Add a first-time result to the collection and increase the discovery count.

The near-term goal is to complete the 92-item Classic collection. Progress is shown through new items, the discovery count, collection/codex surfaces, and workshop presentation milestones. There is no coin economy, producer/idle loop, building upgrade tree, quest campaign, leaderboard, or social feature in the current core loop. The strongest satisfying beat is inferred from the designed sequence: two selected items animate over the pads, then the new result appears and the collection count moves. We do not have player analytics or user research to validate that hypothesis yet.

## Positioning

**Positioning:** A cozy 3D alchemy puzzle where players combine two elements at a workbench to uncover a finite collection of discoveries.

**One-second read:** Combine elements. Discover what comes next.

**Distinctive proof:** Unlike flat list-first alchemy games, this game puts the experiment on a visible 3D workbench with two glowing pads, orbiting element tokens, and a character in the workshop.

**Do not position as:** a conventional same-item merge game, an idle/producer game, a town-builder, an infinite sandbox, a mission/quest game, or a social competition. Those expectations do not match the current playable experience.

## Category recommendation

- Google Play: **Game → Puzzle**. It is the closest supported category for a two-item recipe/discovery loop; add only tags that are clearly supported by the final game and visible in the current console list.
- Apple App Store: **Games → Puzzle**.

## Store constraints and asset standards

- Google Play app title ≤30 characters, short description ≤80, full description ≤4,000; required 512 × 512 store icon and 1024 × 500 feature graphic. Use accurate screenshots of the live app; keep overlay copy brief and localized.
- Apple app name and subtitle are each ≤30 characters; the keywords field is ≤100 characters. Apple product page screenshots support up to 10 images, and 6.9-inch iPhone screenshots include 1320 × 2868, 1290 × 2796, or 1260 × 2736. These assets use 1320 × 2868.
- App previews are optional; Apple supports 15–30 seconds. The supplied storyboard uses captured gameplay and only shows supported interactions.
- Apple product page optimization can compare up to three treatments; each treatment needs real product-page assets and app icons must also be included in the submitted binary.

## Naming recommendation

Recommended working brand: **Curio Alchemy**. English Google Play title: **Curio Alchemy: Element Merge**. The localized Turkish title is **Curio Simya: Element Oyunu**. “Curio” gives the identity a curiosity cue; “Alchemy/Simya” states category; the suffix explains the action. This is a strategic recommendation, not a legal or store-availability clearance. Search trademarks and exact/similar store names in launch territories before adopting it.
